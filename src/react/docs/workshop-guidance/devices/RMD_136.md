# 136: Enrollment Restrictions

**Implementation Effort:** Medium — the settings are simple to configure, but good restriction design depends on your join, enrollment, and exception processes.

**User Impact:** Medium — well-designed restrictions prevent unsupported devices from onboarding, but poor communication can increase help desk volume.

## Overview
Enrollment restrictions in Microsoft Intune help define which platforms, ownership models, and device volumes are allowed into management. In a Zero Trust program, they are part of the front door: they reduce accidental or unauthorized enrollment paths and help make sure devices use the organization's intended onboarding methods, such as Windows Autopilot, Apple Automated Device Enrollment, or Android Enterprise.

## Implementation Guidance
- Define which platforms and ownership scenarios your organization supports, and separate corporate-owned onboarding from personally owned or partner-managed cases.
- Use device platform restrictions to block unsupported platforms and outdated operating system versions from enrolling in Intune in the first place.
- Use device limit restrictions to prevent users from enrolling excessive numbers of devices, especially in shared-account or abuse scenarios.
- Align restrictions with documented enrollment paths such as Autopilot, Apple Automated Device Enrollment, Android Enterprise, and company-owned dedicated device scenarios.
- Create an exception process for legitimate edge cases such as lab devices or transitional migrations, rather than weakening the baseline for everyone.
- Communicate restriction behavior clearly to support teams and end users so blocks are expected and actionable rather than confusing.
