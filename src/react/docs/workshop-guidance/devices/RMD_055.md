# 055: Review provisioning strategy

**Implementation Effort:** High

**User Impact:** High

## Overview
Provisioning strategy determines how quickly devices can be deployed, how much control IT has at first boot, and how consistently security settings are applied. In a Zero Trust device workshop, provisioning should be designed around **ownership**, **platform**, **shared vs. assigned use**, and whether the organization needs day-one compliance and application deployment.

The best strategy is usually not a single method. Most organizations need a small set of approved patterns and a clear rule for when each pattern is used.

## Implementation Guidance
1. Define standard provisioning paths for each device class: corporate-owned assigned, corporate-owned shared, personally owned, and contractor or temporary devices.
2. For Apple corporate-owned devices, prefer **Automated Device Enrollment** through **Apple Business Manager** or **Apple School Manager**. For Apple BYOD, evaluate **account-driven user enrollment** or an app-protection-only approach.
3. For Android, prefer **Android Enterprise** enrollment models that align with ownership and use case. Avoid relying on legacy Android device administrator patterns except where a supported exception clearly exists.
4. Confirm what must happen during provisioning: naming, compliance evaluation, required apps, certificates, Wi-Fi, VPN, and setup experience controls where supported.
5. Decide whether users may self-provision or whether devices must be staged by IT or a partner.
6. Build a pilot matrix that covers first-run sign-in, recovery or re-provisioning, break/fix replacement, and offboarding or wipe.
7. Publish the strategy as approved standards, not as a loose list of options.
