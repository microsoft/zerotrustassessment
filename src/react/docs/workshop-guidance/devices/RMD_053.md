# 053: Review enrolled vs unenrolled for BYOD/Corporate

**Implementation Effort:** Medium to High

**User Impact:** High

## Overview
A Zero Trust device strategy should begin with a clear ownership and management model. The central question is whether a device is **corporate-owned** or **personally owned**, and whether the organization needs **full device management**, **limited enrollment**, or **app-only protection**. The answer affects privacy expectations, what controls can be enforced, and whether access decisions can rely on device compliance.

As a rule, corporate-owned devices should usually be enrolled and managed. Personally owned devices often use either limited enrollment or app-protection-only controls, depending on business need and employee privacy requirements.

## Implementation Guidance
1. Inventory your major device populations: corporate-owned dedicated devices, corporate-owned assigned devices, shared devices, and BYOD.
2. For **corporate-owned** devices, prefer a full enrollment model that supports compliance, configuration, certificates, Wi-Fi, VPN, update management, and remote actions.
3. For **BYOD** devices, decide whether you truly need device enrollment. If the requirement is only secure access to Microsoft 365 data, consider **app protection policies** with Conditional Access instead of full enrollment.
4. For Apple BYOD, compare **account-driven user enrollment** or other supported user-enrollment paths with MAM-only. For Android BYOD, compare **Android Enterprise personally owned work profile** with MAM-only.
5. Document what each model allows: device compliance, Conditional Access based on compliance, certificate delivery, app deployment, remote wipe of work data, and privacy boundaries.
6. Publish a user-facing ownership policy so employees understand what IT can and cannot see or control on personally owned devices.
7. Keep exception cases small and explicit, such as executives, contractors, labs, or regulated frontline workflows.
