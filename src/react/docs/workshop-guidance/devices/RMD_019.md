# 019: Review App config vs App protection policies

**Implementation Effort:** Medium – Requires app inventory, data-protection decisions, and coordination with app owners.

**User Impact:** Medium – Users may see sign-in changes, managed app behaviors, or blocked data-transfer actions depending on policy choices.

## Overview
App configuration policies and app protection policies solve different problems in Microsoft Intune. App configuration policies preconfigure how an app behaves, such as account settings, service endpoints, feature flags, or app-specific options. App protection policies control how work data is accessed, moved, encrypted, or wiped inside supported apps. In Zero Trust, the strongest mobile-app design often uses both: configuration to make the managed experience consistent, and protection to keep corporate data contained.

## Implementation Guidance
1. Use app configuration policies when you want to remove manual setup or standardize how supported apps connect and behave for managed users.
2. Use app protection policies when the requirement is to protect organizational data inside the app, such as requiring a PIN, restricting save-as locations, limiting copy and paste, or enabling selective wipe.
3. For personally owned devices, evaluate whether app protection without full device enrollment is sufficient for the workload. This is a common Zero Trust pattern for mobile BYOD access.
4. For corporate-owned devices, combine device management, app configuration, and app protection where the data sensitivity justifies layered controls.
5. Test the user journey from first launch through sign-in, data sharing, and offboarding. The most common failures happen where app configuration and app protection assumptions do not line up.
6. Document app exceptions explicitly. Some business apps support one policy type better than the other, and unsupported assumptions create rollout delays.
