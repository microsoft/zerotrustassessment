# 152: Organizational messages

**Implementation Effort:** Low to Medium — the technical setup is modest, but communications ownership and targeting discipline are important.

**User Impact:** Low — if used sparingly, organizational messages can improve onboarding and compliance; if overused, they create user fatigue and distrust.

## Overview
Organizational messages can be used to deliver targeted, business-approved communications to managed Windows users, such as reminders related to device setup, self-service actions, Microsoft 365 onboarding, or security tasks. In a Zero Trust program, they can help reinforce desired user behavior at the moment it matters, for example encouraging completion of enrollment steps, restart compliance, or awareness of new device protections. They should be treated as a governed communication channel, not as a general-purpose pop-up system.

## Implementation Guidance
- Define use cases narrowly. Good examples include prompting users to complete onboarding tasks, adopt a new managed app, restart for critical changes, or use a required self-service workflow.
- Assign business ownership for message approval, scheduling, tone, and legal or privacy review where needed. Device admins should not publish user-facing messages without that governance.
- Target messages carefully to the right users and devices so communications are relevant and do not become background noise.
- Do not put sensitive data, incident details, or anything that could create panic or confusion into organizational messages. Use formal incident-communications channels for those cases.
- Pilot on a small audience first and gather help desk feedback to make sure timing, frequency, and wording are effective.
- Measure outcomes if possible, such as completion of an action, reduction in support tickets, or improved adoption of a required control.
