# 112: Teams Devices

**Implementation Effort:** Medium  
**User Impact:** Low to Medium

## Overview

Teams devices such as Teams Rooms systems, Teams Phones, Teams Panels, and Teams Displays should be managed as shared-purpose collaboration endpoints, not as ordinary user devices. In Zero Trust terms, these devices need strong account hygiene, locked-down app and settings scope, controlled update channels, and clear separation of responsibilities between the Microsoft Intune admin center and the Teams admin center.

## Implementation Guidance

1. Catalog the Teams device types in scope and map each one to its supported management path. Teams Rooms on Windows, Android-based Teams devices, and peripheral-attached room systems do not all share the same enrollment and policy model.
2. Use dedicated resource or device accounts where required, and protect those identities with appropriate sign-in controls, break-glass procedures, and password or certificate governance. These accounts are often overlooked because the device feels shared.
3. Apply only the settings the collaboration scenario needs. Kiosk behavior, browser lockdown, USB control, local admin reduction, and network segmentation are usually more important than broad user personalization controls.
4. Coordinate update policy between Intune and Teams-specific device management features. Define who owns app updates, OS updates, device health monitoring, and replacement decisions.
5. Pilot in real meeting rooms or collaboration spaces. Validate sign-in persistence, calendar access, peripheral compatibility, restart windows, and recovery steps after patching.
6. Create a support runbook for room failures, account lockouts, and emergency re-provisioning.
