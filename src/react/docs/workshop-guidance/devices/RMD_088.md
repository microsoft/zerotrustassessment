# 088: Conditional Access policy

**Implementation Effort:** Medium  
**User Impact:** High

## Overview
Conditional Access in Microsoft Entra ID evaluates signals such as user, app, sign-in risk, device state, location, and authentication strength to determine whether access should be allowed, blocked, or subject to additional controls.
For device security workshops, the most important patterns are requiring a compliant device, requiring approved client apps or app protection on BYOD, and layering MFA or stronger authentication for sensitive access. Conditional Access is where Zero Trust policy becomes an actual gate.

## Implementation Guidance
1. Define the protected resources first: Microsoft 365, private web apps, admin portals, or selected cloud apps. Different resources often justify different device requirements.
2. Use a phased rollout. Build the policy in report-only mode first, review impact in sign-in logs and the Conditional Access insights workbook, then enforce after validation.
3. For corporate or enrolled-device scenarios, require the device to be marked as compliant. For BYOD app-only scenarios, consider controls such as approved client app or app protection policy instead of full device compliance.
4. Maintain emergency-access exclusions and document them carefully. Zero Trust requires resilience as well as restriction.
5. Avoid stacking unnecessary controls that create user friction without reducing real risk. Use the minimum set that enforces business and regulatory requirements.
6. Review policy results continuously after deployment, especially after major enrollment, app, or operating system changes.
